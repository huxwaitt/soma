# Why the blocks look like this — five findings and the numbers

Each preference key in `Preferences.md` stands on one of these; change the key, not the rule.

1. **A plan with a when and a where beats an intention.** Gollwitzer and Sheeran's meta-analysis of 94 studies (2006) found that "if-then" plans — this task, that day, that hour — raise the rate at which a goal is reached by a medium-to-large effect (d ≈ 0.65) over the goal alone. So every block is an appointment with a day, a start, an end and a name, not a to-do line.
2. **Deep work needs long blocks in peak hours.** Cognitive performance varies by roughly 15–30 % across the day (Schmidt, Collette, Cajochen and Peigneux's 2007 review of time-of-day effects), and practice studies since Ericsson's (1993) find sessions of about 90 minutes before quality drops. `focus_block_minutes` (90) is the floor; `peak_hours` (09:00–12:00 by default) is where a focus block goes first, and `focus_blocks_per_day` (2) caps them.
3. **Shallow work in a few batches, not all day.** Leroy (2009) showed attention residue: work on the next task suffers while the last one is unfinished. Mark, Gudith and Klocke (2008) measured a mean of 23 minutes to get back to interrupted work. Kushlev and Dunn (2015) found that checking email a few set times a day rather than whenever lowers daily stress. Hence `admin_blocks_per_day` (2) of `admin_block_minutes` (45), outside peak hours, one before and one after lunch.
4. **A fifth of the day stays free.** Porter and Nohria (Harvard Business Review, 2018) tracked 27 chief executives for 13 weeks: 72 % of their work time went to meetings, and the ones who coped kept about 20–25 % of their day unscheduled for what comes up. `slack_share` (0.2) is that share: bookable minutes = (1 − slack) × work minutes − meeting minutes, and a day with nothing left gets no block rather than a smaller slack.
5. **What you measure, you can move.** Drucker's first rule for the effective executive (1967): record where the time goes before deciding where it should go; diary studies (Robinson et al., 2011) find self-estimates of long workweeks off by 20 hours or more. `vault_time_block(action="audit")` counts the week's hours by kind against `Priorities.md`, `/administrator:collect-information` asks each day whether the blocks were held, moved or skipped, and `/administrator:weekly` prints the result under `## Time`.
